# HANDOVER MASTER — GIADA, FONT OF HOPE

## 1. Deck identity

**Commander:** Giada, Font of Hope

**Format:** Commander / Mono-White

**Core identity defined by the owner:** **Angel Tribal + Protection + Resilience**.

The deck is not intended to be generic Lifegain Angels or generic +1/+1 Counters Angels. Lifegain and counters are supporting mechanisms when they help the primary plan.

### Primary game plan

1. Cast as many Angels as possible, preferably through Giada and cost reducers.
2. Build a board of Angels whose power grows naturally through Giada and tribal effects.
3. Create Angel tokens repeatedly or explosively.
4. Protect the board from wipes, preferably by phasing out / granting indestructible / making the board difficult to answer.
5. Use removal and selective board resets so the desired end state is **our creatures remain while opponents lose theirs**.
6. Finish with a wide, highly powered flying board.

## 4. Why these upgrades

**Lyra, Archangel of Dawn:** three-mana Angel that turns life gain into +1/+1 counters across the Angel team. It improves tribal power without forcing the deck to become a lifegain deck.

**Metropolis Reformer:** three-mana Angel that gives the controller hexproof and carries vigilance. It is directly aligned with resilience.

**Resplendent Marshal:** three-mana Angel whose enter/death value can convert an exiled creature from a graveyard into +1/+1 counters on the team.

**Angel of Finality:** four-mana Angel that doubles as graveyard interaction on ETB.

**Angelic Field Marshal:** four-mana Angel that becomes a 5/5 under Giada and grants vigilance to the team while the commander is controlled.

**Folk Hero:** two-mana draw engine; when Giada is the commander, casting an Angel can draw a card once each turn. This is a much tighter fit than generic attack-draw plus Knight tokens.

**Urza's Incubator:** direct acceleration for the stated plan: Angel spells cost two less.

**Lightning Greaves:** zero-equip protection for Giada and other key permanents.

**Swords to Plowshares / Path to Exile:** clean one-mana interaction that lowers the curve while improving the ability to keep opponents' key creatures off the board.

**Entreat the Angels:** scalable Angel-token explosion and a natural late-game mana sink.

**Guardian of Faith:** the deliberate non-Angel exception. Flash + mass phase-out directly implements the resilience objective.

## 5. Why the cuts

The cuts are not a declaration that these cards are 'bad Magic cards'. They are cuts **for this deck**.

- **Herald of Eternal Dawn:** 8 MV and not enough immediate impact for the slot.
- **The Immortal Sun:** 6 MV generic value; weak tribal identity.
- **Angelic Skirmisher:** good combat flexibility, but six mana is expensive for a support Angel.
- **Sanctuary Warden:** six-mana value card whose output is less direct than the new 3–4 MV Angel engines.
- **Angel of Destiny:** moves the deck toward lifegain/alternate-win rather than Angel-tribal resilience.
- **The Book of Exalted Deeds:** cute Faceless Haven package, but occupies a slot without materially increasing tribal resilience.
- **Dowsing Dagger:** conditional combat-based ramp with tempo friction.
- **Everybody Lives!:** protects opponents as well, making it less attractive than board-preserving protection.
- **Split Up:** useful wipe, but does not protect our board; it is better treated as a backup option.
- **Vanquish the Horde:** eight-mana ceiling and symmetrical destruction conflict with the desired end state.
- **Chivalric Alliance:** cheap, but the Knight-token side and generic attack-draw are less tribal than Folk Hero.
- **The One Ring:** strong generic card draw/protection, but the slot is more valuable as dedicated tribal speed/resilience.

## 9. Card philosophy for future additions

When a new card is proposed, ask in this order:

**A. Does it improve Angel density or Angel output?**

**B. Does it make the deck faster?**

**C. Does it help our board survive?**

**D. Does it create or reuse Angel bodies?**

**E. Does it help remove opposing creatures while preserving our board?**

**F. If it is a non-Angel, is the benefit extraordinary enough to justify losing a tribal slot?**

A generic powerful card should still be rejected when the answer is merely 'it is good'.

## 13. Recommended next step

Use V2 as the baseline and then perform a **full 99-card micro-audit** after a few games: identify which cards actually feel slow, which protection pieces are stranded, how often Angel tokens are generated, and whether the 35 mana sources feel correct at the table. Future upgrades should be evaluated against this baseline rather than against generic Angel-staple lists.
